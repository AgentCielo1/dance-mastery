# African Dance Partnerships — The Plan

Why this document exists: the industry research (Doc 02 §3) was unambiguous about how
African dance content goes wrong online — non-African re-teachers out-earning
originators, steps stripped of history, lineages bypassed. The practitioner community's
own stated norms are equally clear: **credit the culture and lineage publicly, seek
permission from tradition-bearers, hire and pay African instructors, ship context with
steps.** This platform committed to that architecture from Doc 01. This is how we
execute it.

## Current state (honest inventory)

| Pack | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Afrobeats** | Shipped v0.1 — named moves with creators credited, history nodes, conservative attributions | The named-move canon (Azonto, Shaku Shaku, Zanku, Pilolo…) is public, widely documented vocabulary; social dances aren't copyrightable; creators are named in the data and tests enforce it. Still a *candidate for upgrade*: the goal is Ghanaian/Nigerian dancers replacing our stylized placeholders with their own captured motion, under their names, for revenue share. |
| **West African (Guinea/Mali)** | Shipped as FOUNDATION ONLY — rhythm literacy, break-ear training, class ritual, grounded posture. **The dance repertoire per rhythm is deliberately absent.** | These dances live in lineages (the teaching tradition descending from Les Ballets Africains through masters like Youssouf Koumbassa and Mouminatou Camara). Authoring their steps from videos without a tradition-bearer would be extraction. The pack says so in its own curriculum (`meta.lineage_honesty`). |
| **South & Central African** (amapiano, pantsula, gumboot, ndombolo) | **Not authored at all — by decision.** | Entirely creator-led scenes with no structured platform anywhere (the largest open gap the research found). Building it *without* those creators would repeat the exact pattern the culture resents. Building it *with* them is the opportunity. |

## The partnership model (what we offer, concretely)

For every partnered dancer/teacher/school:

1. **Authorship, on-screen.** Their name on every move they contribute — in the skill
   tree data itself (the `origin`/`teachers` fields the app renders), not a credits page.
2. **Revenue share on their pack.** A defined percentage of any future revenue
   attributable to the style pack they author or capture for. (Platform is pre-revenue;
   the term sheet locks the split *now* so it's never renegotiated from weakness.)
3. **Capture on their terms.** Paid session rates for motion capture, the Doc 06 §2
   performer agreement (they keep name/likeness control; stylized avatar by default),
   and approval rights over every clip and checkpoint published under their name.
4. **Context in their words.** History/culture nodes authored or approved by them —
   we provide the format, they provide the truth.
5. **Their channels win first.** Every lesson's "Watch the real thing" panel links to
   *their* platforms — the app feeds their audience, never substitutes for it.

## Target partners (from the research, in priority order)

- **Afrobeats (upgrade path):** DWP Academy (Accra) and its orbit — the flagship
  Afrodance school (Dancegod Lloyd, co-founder); creators of named moves such as
  Incredible Zigi. Model: capture jams in Accra or with diaspora dancers; per-move
  authorship + pack revenue share.
- **West African (unlock path):** teachers in the Guinean stage lineage — the
  Koumbassa/Camara school of pedagogy (Cumbe Center, Brooklyn is the natural door in
  the US). Model: the teacher defines the per-rhythm movement curriculum; we build it
  into nodes under their name; live-class referrals built in.
- **South/Central African (creation path):** creator-teachers already teaching online —
  the CiiCii model for Congolese ndombolo (Congolese Dance Tutorials), pantsula crews,
  amapiano step creators. Model: each style pack is *their* pack on our engine —
  authored, fronted, and revenue-shared by them.

## Sequencing

1. Now → first capture weekends: the founder's own breaking/hip hop content proves the
   pipeline end-to-end (capture → cleanup → lesson → scoring refs) with zero third-party
   risk.
2. Then → one pilot partnership (most accessible: a diaspora Afrodance or West African
   teacher within travel distance) using the terms above; their pack ships as the
   template.
3. Then → remote capture kits (the Doc 09 playbook + phone rig ships as instructions) for
   partners abroad; DWP-style school partnerships follow the pilot's proven terms.

## The line we hold

Until a partnership exists for a tradition: rhythm literacy, history, posture, and
class-readiness can ship (public knowledge, honestly framed); the dances themselves
wait. An empty node with a named plan is worth more than a full node that steals.
